Capture the CT film

Photograph the film on a lightbox or bright screen, straight on, filling the frame. Turn off the flash — glare destroys grey–white differentiation.

Getting a usable photo

  • Backlight, don't flash. Lightbox or a white full-screen laptop background behind the film.
  • Shoot square on. Keep the sensor parallel to the film; step 2 can only fix mild keystone.
  • One level per capture. Score the ganglionic and supraganglionic levels separately.
  • Prefer the DICOM window. A photo of a monitor at stroke window (W35–40/L35–40) beats a photo of printed film.

Align the slice

Drag the four handles to the corners of the slice you want to score. This removes keystone distortion so left and right hemispheres are geometrically comparable.

Window the image

A photograph has no Hounsfield units, so this is a display-contrast stretch, not true windowing. It can reveal loss of grey–white differentiation that survived the print, but it cannot recover data the film discarded.

Score the 10 regions

ASPECTS is scored on the affected hemisphere only. Subtract one point for each region showing early ischaemic change — hypoattenuation or focal swelling with loss of grey–white differentiation. Compare against the mirror-image region on the normal side.

    NIH Stroke Scale

    Fifteen items, total 0–42. Score what you see, not what you think the patient can do. Administer items in order, do not go back and change scores, and do not coach the patient.

    Ground rules from the NIH scale instructions. Record answers while administering the exam and work quickly. Except where indicated, the patient should not be coached. If the patient gives up or refuses, the score for that item is the deficit observed. Items marked UN (untestable — amputation, joint fusion, intubation) contribute 0 to the total and are flagged separately in the report.

    Image quality check

      This is a technical check of the photograph only — sharpness, resolution, exposure, glare and colour cast. It says nothing about the CT itself and never looks for ischaemia.

      About Neuro App CHSC

      Two bedside tools in one offline-capable web app: a camera front end for manual ASPECTS scoring of non-contrast head CT, and a structured NIHSS assessment with a running total.

      What the ASPECTS tool does

      Capture, perspective correction, contrast stretching and local contrast enhancement, structured region marking on a schematic, score arithmetic, and an exportable record.

      What it deliberately does not do

      It does not detect ischaemia. A photograph of a film has already been windowed, gamma-corrected and JPEG-compressed, so the 2–4 HU attenuation drop that defines early ischaemic change is largely gone. Automated readers work on DICOM volumes and still only reach moderate-to-good agreement with experts. Any auto-scoring claim here would be unsafe and would also make the app a regulated device.

      Region set

      Ganglionic level: caudate (C), lentiform (L), internal capsule (IC), insula (I), M1, M2, M3. Supraganglionic level: M4, M5, M6. Each abnormal region subtracts one point from 10.

      NIHSS notes

      Fifteen items, 0–42. Untestable items contribute 0 and are flagged. A low total does not exclude stroke — posterior circulation and lacunar events can score very low, and the scale weights left-hemisphere deficits more heavily.

      Suggested validation study

      Two readers score the same case set on paper and in the app; report ICC and weighted kappa for app-vs-paper and reader-vs-reader agreement, plus time-to-score. That is a publishable methods paper.
